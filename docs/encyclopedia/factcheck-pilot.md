## what-does-the-speed-bar-do-on-a-paraglider
1. **MEDIUM:** "The Hook 6's certification gives it about 11 km/h more from the bar than from the brakes alone [3]".
   - **Evidence:** 25 minus 14, unlabelled, and it reads as "bar range = brake range + 11".
   - **Fix:** "As a worked example from its certification figures (14 km/h on the brakes, 25 in all), the bar adds about 11 km/h above trim."
2. **MEDIUM (safety wording):** "The manuals agree on four rules: Do not fly accelerated close to the ground or in turbulent air [2][5][1]".
   - **Evidence:** Gin says "use caution in turbulence". Gradient says "very carefully, or not at all at low altitude". Rule 4 comes from Ozone only.
   - **Fix:** "The manuals give four rules", and quote each maker's position accurately.
3. **MEDIUM (safety clarity):** Rule 4 tells you to control the wing with the rear risers while on bar [5][9], but a later line says "the speed bar must come off before any C-riser input [9]". Read together, these contradict each other.
   - **Evidence:** In LM7 (fetched) that line is about a nose collapse while accelerated: "release the speed bar impulsively ... always release the speed bar first before making any C inputs".
   - **Fix:** "On bar, hold the rear risers. If the nose starts to fold, release the bar first, then make small C inputs."
4. **MEDIUM (show, Tom c12):** "...which is exactly why brakes and bar do not mix."
   - **Evidence:** Tom does not say this. He also says the brake input is "reducing" the pitch-up tendency, not cancelling it.
   - **Fix:** Cut the clause, and change "cancels" to "reduces".
5. **LOW (show, Luc c2):** "14 cm of speed system travel".
   - **Evidence:** The body text says 140 mm of riser shortening.
   - **Fix:** "14 cm of riser shortening".

The 145 mm, 18 cm and 140 mm figures are correct. The figure matches Belcourt c8, and Schrempf c3 is supported.

## what-happens-if-a-paraglider-collapses
1. **HIGH (safety omission):** "...deep pumps on that side while leaning away. A full stall is the last resort [1][7]."
   - **Evidence:** The Delta 4 manual says of the full stall: "Only attempt this if you have enough altitude and you know what you are doing." It also says "if the rotation is accelerating and you are unable to control it, throw your reserve parachute immediately", and warns that brake inputs "may stall the opposite wing".
   - **Fix:** "Ozone lists a full stall as the next option for a very large cravat, only with height and if you know how. If the rotation speeds up and you cannot control it, throw the reserve at once [1]."
2. **MEDIUM (show, Schrempf c4):** "When Helmut Schrempf feels one side drop, he releases the tension in that hip..."
   - **Evidence:** The chapter is about sensing a side go light on bar in rough air, before anything folds. Beside "Lean away from the collapse", the card reads as a contradiction.
   - **Fix:** Start the card with "On bar in rough air, before anything has folded,".
3. **LOW:** "If you were on the speed bar, release it first [2]" comes after the pumping advice.
   - **Evidence:** Gin says to release the bar "immediately, then apply the standard recovery technique".
   - **Fix:** Move it to the start of that paragraph.

Gin Seok Song c10, Tom c5 and Zoller c9 are supported.

## what-is-a-stall-on-a-paraglider
1. **HIGH (safety):** "recovery always starts the same way: by reducing the angle of attack [2]".
   - **Evidence:** AC 61-67C is about aeroplanes. Gin, on this page: "brakes must be held until it moves overhead again" and "Never release a spin when the glider is far behind you". "Always" could lead a pilot to release a full stall at the wrong moment.
   - **Fix:** "For aircraft, the FAA says recovery means reducing the angle of attack. On a paraglider that means letting the brakes up, but only in the controlled way described below."
2. **MEDIUM:** The short answer says a stall "almost always means too much brake", and the body says "the brakes are what raise the angle of attack".
   - **Evidence:** It conflicts with the page's own causes (rear risers [9], big ears, wet wing, B-stall release) and Tom's B-riser card.
   - **Fix:** "usually too much brake, though rear-riser input, a wet wing or lines out of trim can also cause it".
3. **LOW (show, Tom c9 and c13):** The cards say "back-fly and recover", "visibly deforms" and "far less pressure".
   - **Evidence:** He says "backfly parachute and stall", "deforms accordingly" and "not going to be as high".
   - **Fix:** Match his wording.

The Tom c12 card is supported (at least 7 cm of brake travel, no tension at full bar).
