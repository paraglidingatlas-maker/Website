Overview

The Land Of Five Hills

Panchgani sits on the Sahyadri, the range that runs down India's west coast about 100 km inland, on the volcanic tablelands of the Deccan: flat tops and sharp vertical cliffs. Vistasp Kharas grew up here and has flown it for more than ten years. He calls it far more technical than Bir: a 30 km flight in Panchgani is as good as 100 km in Bir Billing.

The height

Take-off sits at about 1,100 to 1,200 m, and in the peak of the season pilots climb to 3,000 to 4,000 m, so on a good day the height above the ground beats Bir's.

The air

The cliffs heat through the morning and throw up strong thermals. There are inversions at several layers, the wind can change direction every thousand metres, and each valley and bowl has its own wind.

The window

Every evening the sea breeze comes in from the coast, so the flying day is timed around it. In the time you get for cross country, it is glorious.

The season

November to March. The wind starts from the north-northeast and turns southeast towards spring, so the take-off is chosen to match. The tour flies in February, in the heart of it.

Panchgani held its first pre World Cup in 2019, with almost 100 pilots, and its second, the first run by the Paragliding Association of India, in 2024. The longest local line runs about 100 km north west to Kamshet.

01

Who is this tour for?

Pilots who can take off in strong wind and strong thermic conditions. Vistasp Kharas, who has flown Panchgani for more than ten years, advises an SIV course: the thermals can be punchy and there is wind in the mix. He would not send a pilot used only to alpine air with little knowledge of wind, because on the lee side the site is not forgiving.

02

What wing should I bring?

A high B or a C is great, and even a low B does well; an A will be a bit frustrating. Do not bring your best wing: the take-off at Harrison's Folly is red dust, and the local pilots keep a second wing that is already red.

03

How high does it get?

Take-off is at about 1,100 to 1,200 m, and in the peak of the season pilots climb to 3,000 to 4,000 m. The local rule: below 1,800 m, start looking for a safe landing, and stay off the terrain unless you know exactly what the wind is doing.

04

Where do we land?

Top landing on the main tableland is forgiving late in the afternoon, once you know where the wind is from. In the valleys, land near a road in a field that has been cut: never standing crops or sugarcane. The power lines are above ground, so look for the poles and their shadows. The villagers are friendly, and a shared jeep back to town is cheap.

05

How do I get there?

Panchgani is about two hours by road from Pune and four and a half to five hours from Mumbai. No train goes to Panchgani: take the train from Mumbai to Pune, about four hours, then a taxi or a bus.
