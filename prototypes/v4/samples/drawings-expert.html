The drawings, three ways

Three knowledge base drawings as a mathematician, a painter and a visualisation specialist would make them. Pick a direction before the rest are redrawn.

Mathematician and scientist

Flight Mechanics: the brakes, computed

Today's drawing illustrates the idea with hand-drawn airflow. Here the same profile at the same three brake positions is put through a real calculation: the streamlines, the pressure on the surface (orange where it sucks, grey where it presses) and the point where the lift acts all come out of it. It agrees with the episode: as the brake comes on, the lift grows and moves back along the chord, if by less than today's drawing suggests.

Painter

Meteorology: the low and its front, painted

The same subject, composed as a picture: one light, one focal point, the only colour on the front. Tonal bands deepen toward the centre of the low; the lines are heavier close in and fade with distance; the isobars bend where they cross the front, as they do on a real chart, and the front's triangles point the way it moves.

Visualisation specialist

Navigators: the map, equal area and made for a phone

Today's map uses a stretched rectangle, where the far north swells and Africa looks small, and on a phone it shrinks until the names can't be read. Here it is on an Equal Earth map, which keeps every country's area true, with the graticule curving as the projection does; on a phone it becomes its own drawing, numbered, with the names in a list you can read.
