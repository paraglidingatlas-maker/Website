Most pilots choose a harness by how it feels on a two hour flight. Zsolt Ero argues you should be choosing it for the four seconds that decide whether you walk away.

In this episode we go through back protector types and what the certification actually measures, why an airbag is weakest at exactly the moment you are most likely to need it, how competition standards are changing, and the three questions worth asking before any harness purchase.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the safest paragliding harness back protector?

No single type wins outright. Solid foam and engineered structures such as Koroyd provide protection immediately, including at launch and landing where airbags may not be fully inflated. Certified protection of any type is substantially better than none.

Do I need a back protector for cross country flying?

Yes. Cross country flying involves more time over unlandable terrain and more launches and landings in unfamiliar places, which is where impact protection matters most.

Where should a reserve parachute be mounted?

Wherever you can reach the handle instantly without looking for it, while under load and wearing gloves. Front mount, side mount and under seat all work if that condition is met.
