# Legal review brief: Paragliding Atlas policy documents

**Purpose of this file.** Four documents are live on the site. They were drafted
carefully but not by a lawyer. This brief exists so a review costs one focused
hour instead of several exploratory ones: it says what was drafted, what was
deliberately decided, and the six questions actually worth paying for.

Give this file to the reviewer along with the four URLs.

## Decisions already taken, and the reasoning

**1. No negligence waiver.** The reference document supplied was Trek Travel's
US participant waiver, in which the customer releases the operator from its own
negligence under Wisconsin law. That was deliberately NOT copied. A term
excluding liability for death or personal injury caused by negligence is not
binding in Norway or the EEA, and including one would be both unenforceable and
a bad signal to an informed customer. The documents instead use assumption of
inherent risk plus participant responsibility, and say openly that no negligence
waiver is being sought.

**2. No fourteen day withdrawal right.** Relied on Article 16(l) of the Consumer
Rights Directive: leisure services supplied on a specific date are exempt. Stated
plainly rather than left silent.

**3. Package travel framing.** Terms section 2 describes the customer's rights
under Directive (EU) 2015/2302 and the Norwegian Package Travel Act, but does
NOT claim the business holds insolvency protection, because that was unconfirmed
at the time of writing. See question 1 below.

**4. Commercial terms are now the operator's own, not defaults.** An earlier
draft carried invented defaults (25 per cent deposit, balance at 60 days, a
90/60/30 sliding scale). Those were wrong and have been replaced from the
operator's own documents: deposit with registration, full balance 120 days
before departure, and NO refunds on deposit or balance, including where the tour
does not meet its flight objectives and including participants who leave early.

**5. Privacy and cookie notices were rewritten against the code, not the drafts.**
The supplied drafts described Klarna, GoDaddy, Cookiebot, Google Analytics 4 and
Leaflet Maps. None are present on the site. It is hosted on GitHub Pages, has no
analytics, no consent platform and no payment processor, and no code on it writes
a cookie or uses browser storage. The published notices describe what the site
actually loads: GitHub Pages, Google Fonts, YouTube in nocookie mode, cdnjs,
unpkg, and the operator's own Cloudflare Worker.

## The six questions worth paying for

**1. Is the termination fee mechanism the right call, and is the deposit
defensible?**
The operator's own document says no refunds at all. That was published, then
redrafted, because Article 23 of Directive (EU) 2015/2302 makes a term waiving
the traveller's rights non-binding: the clause would simply fail, and take the
credibility of the rest of the document with it. An unenforceable term protects
nobody.

The page now uses the statutory mechanism from Article 12(1) instead of a
percentage scale: the termination fee is the trip price, less genuine cost
savings, less income from reselling the place, with the calculation shown on
request. Where costs really are committed and the place cannot be resold that
approaches the full price, so the commercial protection is largely preserved
while the term stays enforceable. No percentages were invented to achieve it.

Questions for review: is a non-refundable deposit defensible as an appropriate
and justifiable fee at the moment of booking, and would the operator prefer a
published standardised scale, which Article 12(1) also permits and which is
easier for a customer to understand, over the case-by-case calculation used here?
Also confirm the weather carve-out holds: the page says the traveller is buying
guiding and access rather than a guaranteed number of flights, so weather is not
a failure to perform and triggers no price reduction under Articles 13 and 14.

**2. Is this a package, and is insolvency protection in place?**
The trips are guided, multi day, and may be sold with accommodation and transfers.
If they are packages under the Package Travel Act, Norwegian law requires
insolvency protection, in practice registration with Reisegarantifondet, and the
standard information form must be given to the customer before booking. This is
the only item in this brief with a regulatory consequence for trading, and it
should be answered first.

**3. Are the payment deadlines enforceable as drafted?**
Under Norwegian and EU consumer law a cancellation charge must reflect a genuine
estimate of the cost the operator cannot recover, not act as a penalty. Does the
90/60/30 scale survive that test for a trip of this cost and lead time, and is
the "we will refund what we recover if we resell your place" clause enough to
support it?

**4. Does the risk framing hold up if it is ever tested?**
Assumption of inherent risk is the mechanism used throughout. Is it drafted
strongly enough to be meaningful, while staying clearly on the right side of the
prohibition on excluding liability for personal injury caused by negligence?
The pilot in command principle in participant agreement section 2 is doing a lot
of work and is the clause most likely to be argued about.

**4. Is the mandatory insurance condition enforceable?**
Participation is conditional on holding cover that includes helicopter rescue and
repatriation, and the operator may refuse participation without proof. Is
refusal without refund defensible where a participant turns up uninsured?

**5. Is the health data handling proportionate?**
Health information is special category data under Article 9. The stated basis is
explicit consent, plus vital interests in an emergency, retained only for the
duration of the trip. Is consent the right basis here given the imbalance between
operator and participant, and is the retention period defensible?

**6. Minors.**
Both documents assume parent or guardian signature for under 18s but the trips
have a minimum flying rating that most minors would not hold. Should under 18s be
excluded outright, which would simplify several clauses?

## Known gaps, already identified, not legal questions

- **`enquire.html` does not exist.** The nav links to it from every page. It will
  become the main data collection point, so its form fields and consent wording
  should be drafted alongside the privacy notice rather than after it.
- **Google Fonts** loads from Google on every page, transferring visitor IP to a
  US company. Self hosting the two font files removes the transfer entirely and
  is the cleanest available privacy improvement. Roughly twenty minutes of work.
- **`Policies.pdf`** in the client's Drive folder could not be read: it is a scan
  with no text layer. If it contains material terms, it needs OCR and may change
  what is above.
- **No standard information form** for package travel has been produced yet. Its
  wording is prescribed by Annex I of the Directive and should be drafted once
  question 1 is answered.
